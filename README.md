Applying to your ideal job after graduating can be stressful as a lot of students feel they are underqualified.
Often times it takes more than just the base major course track to catch the attention of hiring managers.
This work presents some ideas to boost your exprience and resume to better appeal to your career of choice.

___
## Extracurriculars
Participating in extracurricular activities on campus opens up broad oppurtunities like getting hands-on 
experience, becoming involved in your community, and networking with mentors.

#### On-Campus Events
---
All UW campuses provide on-campus events like mentorship nights, skill workshops, employer tours, and career presentations. 
Attending these events can boost necessities like your resume, give you an idea of what a job in that area might look like, 
and gives you the skills you need to prepare for your future career. 

You can find more information on the campus event schedule through the [event page](https://www.uwb.edu/calendar), the school paper,
or posters around campus.

#### Clubs
---
There are countless clubs you can find on the club registry page that are always accepting new members.
Joining a club or two relating to your area of interest (ex. engineering --> robotics) gives you
experience in your field and becomes a stand out quality to recruiters.

Check the [club registry](https://gather.uwb.edu/club_signup) and scout out which catagories interest you. Be sure not to overload your schedule.

___
## Electives
Elective classes are required for your major. However, relating those electives to your interests and goals rather than
choosing blindly can prepare you for what kinds of projects you might take on in the future.

#### University-Sanctioned Programs
---
Programs like [study abroad](https://www.uwb.edu/connected-learning/global/abroad), research labs, and company-sponsored electives can count towards your elective credits. For example, if you are wanting to work in medical technology development, start messaging research groups or professors about medical device labs. These programs generally provide on-site expereiences that you may use later on, alongside future mentors you can use as a reference.

Attend meetings and talk with professors about what kinds of programs or research they sponser. 

___
## Internships
Applying and working as an intern is one of the best ways to ensure you land your ideal job. Internships prove to recruiters that you've
already had background in your area. These also give you the chance to stay at a company you intern at if you mesh well with their workspace. 
Networking is one of the best benefits of interning, as you get to meet with many different people during your time at the company. Students
who intern are far more likely to be offered full-time positions when applying to long-term careers [^1].

Work on your LinkedIn and resume to begin applying to internships on various job websites. It will take multiple tries, don't be discouraged 
by your first rejection.

#### Tailoring Applications
---
Editing your cover letter and resume to better fit the job expectations on the employers site gives you a better chance of being reviewed.
Rather than mass sending your base documents, take some time to tailor them to the career you're applying for. This involves emphasizing 
relevant job experience, adjusting your resume summary, and using similar language to the posting.

Read over the job description and stylize your documents to match the expectations of your potential employer.

## Strategies for Success
Utilize what's around you and what you're already involved in to enhance your prospects. Prioritize what strategies work best for you
and what would be most important to your future career. 

#### Career Services
---
UW campuses offer resources like a [career services office](https://www.uwb.edu/career-services/) 
to help build resumes, gain interview skills, and get guidance on what kinds of programs would
best fit you. They often hold events and coaching resources for students to frequently attend.

Work with career services to build your resume. You can have all the experience, but the most important
part is being able to relay that to an employer.

#### Networking Websites
---
Websites like LinkedIn, [Husky Handshake](https://careers.uw.edu/resources/handshake/), and alumni associations are
great ways to connect with others in your fields, experienced graduates similar to you, and companies you'd like 
to apply for. Careers and internships are posted on LinkedIn and Husky Handshake, which make them great ways to find opportunities.

Create and spend some time on these profiles. Keep track of companies, disciplines, and other peers by connecting with them
through these platforms.

#### Attaining Leadership Roles
---
Participating in clubs and activities are already good ways to boost your resumes. However, you can upgrade these roles by
becoming a leader in your area. Leadership skills and taking the initiative are always great qualities that hiring manangers are on the lookout for.

Try to audition or run for positions in what you are involved in. Even if you don't succeed, you still put yourself out there
and can learn what to improve for next time.
